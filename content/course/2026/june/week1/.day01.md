## A telephone pole casts a 20-foot shadow

## You cast a 5-foot shadow

## How tall is the pole?

*You don't have a ladder. You can't climb it.*

---

## Here's what you know

- You are **5 ft tall**
- Your shadow right now: **4 ft**
- The pole's shadow right now: **20 ft**

At the exact same moment, the sun hits you and the pole at the same angle.

Does that give you enough to find the pole's height?

## The key insight

Because the sun angle is the same for everything at the same moment:

$$\frac{\text{your height}}{\text{your shadow}} = \frac{\text{pole's height}}{\text{pole's shadow}}$$

This is a **proportion** — two ratios that are equal.

---

## Solve it

$$\frac{5}{4} = \frac{?}{20}$$

Cross-multiply:

$$5 \times 20 = 4 \times ? \implies ? = \frac{100}{4} = 25 \text{ ft}$$

The pole is **25 feet tall.**

No ladder required.

## Same idea, completely different situation

A map of Texas has a scale printed in the corner:

**1 inch = 80 miles**

You measure the distance between Austin and El Paso on the map.
It's **4.3 inches.**

How far apart are they in real life?

---

## Map: set it up

The scale gives you one ratio. The unknown distance gives you another.

$$\frac{1 \text{ in}}{80 \text{ mi}} = \frac{4.3 \text{ in}}{? \text{ mi}}$$

$$? = 4.3 \times 80 = 344 \text{ mi}$$

*(Actual distance: ~351 miles. Maps are close, not exact.)*

The math is the same. The situation is completely different.

## One more

Two gears mesh together.

- Small gear: **12 teeth**
- Large gear: **48 teeth**
- Small gear spins: **8 times**

How many times does the large gear spin?

*Hint: every tooth the small gear pushes past the contact point, the large gear receives.*

---

## Gears: set it up

Every turn of the small gear moves 12 teeth. The large gear receives those same teeth.

$$\text{small turns} \times 12 = \text{large turns} \times 48$$

$$8 \times 12 = ? \times 48 \implies ? = \frac{96}{48} = 2 \text{ turns}$$

Or as a proportion:

$$\frac{8}{?} = \frac{48}{12}$$

---

## The concept: Ratio and Proportion

A **ratio** compares two quantities: $\dfrac{a}{b}$

A **proportion** says two ratios are equal: $\dfrac{a}{b} = \dfrac{c}{d}$

You've now seen this in three completely different situations — shadows, maps, gears.

The pattern: **two quantities that scale together at a constant rate.**

To solve for an unknown: **cross-multiply.** $\;a \times d = b \times c$
