# Math Through the Real World — June 2026
**June 2 – June 27, 2026 | ~4 weeks | ~3 sessions/week**

## Student Profile
- Age: 13.5, homeschooled
- Coding: some experience (Python basics — can write working simple programs)
- This month: math-heavy, but every session is anchored in a real phenomenon

## Design Philosophy
Every session opens with something real — a shadow, a bounce, a flood, a coin flip. The math emerges from trying to describe or predict it precisely. Progress through 7th/8th grade math is real but driven by curiosity, not curriculum sequence.

Sessions are never "today we do fractions" — they are "here is something weird; let's measure it and figure out the rule."

## Math Coverage (7th/8th grade)
- Ratios and proportional relationships
- Operations with rational numbers (integers, fractions, negatives)
- Expressions, equations, inequalities (one- and two-step)
- Geometry: area, surface area, volume, similar figures
- The coordinate plane and slope
- Linear functions
- The Pythagorean theorem
- Probability and basic statistics

## Week 1: Shadows & Similar Triangles (Jun 2–6)
*Big question: How tall is something you can't climb?*

| Day | Phenomenon | Math |
|-----|-----------|------|
| 1 | A telephone pole casts a 20-foot shadow. You cast a 5-foot shadow. How tall is the pole? | Ratios, proportional reasoning |
| 2 | Why does the same object cast different-length shadows at 9am vs noon? | Similar triangles, angles, scale |
| 3 | Can you calculate the height of a building from across the street using only a ruler and your eyes? | Proportions, measurement, similar figures |

---

## Week 2: Slope & Falling Things (Jun 9–13)
*Big question: Where will the ball land?*

| Day | Phenomenon | Math |
|-----|-----------|------|
| 1 | Drop a ball off a table — does it fall straight or curve? Why? | Coordinate plane, plotting points |
| 2 | A ramp is 4 feet long and rises 1 foot. How steep is that? What if you double the length? | Slope, rise over run, ratios |
| 3 | A skateboard rolls down a ramp and across the floor. Can you predict where it stops? | Linear functions, writing equations from slope |

---

## Week 3: Volume & Weird Shapes (Jun 16–20)
*Big question: How much water does it actually hold?*

| Day | Phenomenon | Math |
|-----|-----------|------|
| 1 | A fish tank is 24" × 12" × 16". You fill it 3/4 full. How many gallons is that? | Volume, unit conversion, rational numbers |
| 2 | A water tower is a cylinder on legs. City engineers need to know its capacity. | Volume of cylinders, surface area |
| 3 | You're designing a soup can. Same volume, different dimensions — which uses the least metal? | Optimization, surface area vs volume |

---

## Week 4: Chance & Data (Jun 23–27)
*Big question: Is this game fair?*

| Day | Phenomenon | Math |
|-----|-----------|------|
| 1 | Roll two dice and add them. Why does 7 come up more than 2? | Probability, sample spaces, fractions |
| 2 | You flip a coin 100 times and get 62 heads. Is the coin rigged? | Experimental vs theoretical probability, statistics |
| 3 | A basketball player makes 72% of free throws. What are the odds she makes 3 in a row? | Compound probability, multiplication rule |
